01 / README

The mind, not the résumé.

I am difficult to describe by listing what I do, because the list makes it sound sequential. It isn't.

I can be designing an information architecture, helping someone untangle a technical problem, thinking about moral culpability in a sixteenth-century poem, answering a younger engineer who isn't sure where they belong, and noticing that somebody used one particular word strangely three messages ago.

Somewhere underneath all of that, there is probably music playing.

My mind is highly parallel. I don't experience engineering, philosophy, relationships, fiction, technology, faith, and curiosity as sealed compartments. They leak into each other constantly.

An API teaches me something about boundaries. A conversation teaches me something about information architecture. A poem gives me a better model of choice. A badly designed kitchen becomes a systems problem. Someone saying “hmm, explain more” can occupy the same mental landscape as distributed software architecture.

I work at the seam between business processes and software engineering: writing software, reverse-engineering systems, translating between people who know what they need and machines that require considerably more precision. Give me an ambiguous problem with several incompatible constraints and I become considerably happier than is probably normal.

I like systems, but I care about the humans trapped inside them.

A lot of my free time goes into talking with people—often students, younger engineers, or people trying to enter technology—because sometimes someone doesn't need another course or motivational speech. They need twenty minutes with somebody who can see the shape of the problem they are standing in.

I don't particularly want an audience. I'd rather be useful to one actual person.

02 / cognition

How I tend to think.

Parallel

I keep several models alive at once. Subjects do not wait politely in separate queues, and they frequently exchange information.

Structural

I want the architecture underneath the thing: the incentives, assumptions, dependencies, failure modes, and hidden constraints.

Translational

I move between abstraction and implementation easily. A philosophical problem may become an engineering metaphor. An engineering failure may reveal a human one.

Attentive

I notice wording, exceptions, odd details, and the sentence that does not quite fit. The anomaly is often where the useful information is hiding.

04 / human context

There is a person attached to the processor.

I am a Christian, an empiricist, and a rationalist in the broad sense—which means I have had to become comfortable letting convictions survive contact with questions rather than protecting them from questions.

I write fiction. I read old books. I play Commander. I like technology, but I am more interested in what technology does to the shape of human choice than in gadgets for their own sake.

I have spent enough time around complicated things to distrust the instinct to make them simple too quickly. People included. Perhaps especially people.

05 / interface

Conversation protocol.

Bring the real question.
The one underneath the polite version is usually more interesting.

Disagree cleanly.
I am not looking for agreement. I am looking for enough precision that we can locate the actual disagreement.

Do not simplify yourself for me.
Technical, strange, unfinished, contradictory, niche—I would rather have the high-resolution version.

Ask for help if I can actually help.
Computers, AI, software, manufacturing, technical careers, or simply figuring out what question you should be asking.

Rabbit holes are permitted.
There is a non-zero chance your original question will eventually become philosophy. This is considered normal operation.

06 / compressed truth

For a long time I assumed everyone else was doing roughly the same amount of processing and simply talking about less of it. I no longer think that is true.

Speed by itself is cheap. The useful part is holding several models at once, moving between them, noticing when unrelated problems share a structure, and staying with a question long enough for the easy answer to become unsatisfying.

07 / contact

Bring something interesting.

I like perceptive people, difficult questions, strange ideas, and conversations that leave both people somewhere different from where they started.

If you are trying to get into computers, AI, software, or manufacturing and do not know where to begin, ask. If you have an argument you cannot quite resolve, ask. If you have a bizarre question nobody around you wants to entertain, definitely ask.
