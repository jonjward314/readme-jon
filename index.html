01 / A starting point

The compressed
version is
misleading.

I like systems, but I care about the humans inside them.

I build software, reverse-engineer systems, write fiction, and follow questions across disciplines. The work changes. The habit is fairly consistent: look beneath the explanation until the structure begins to show.

An API teaches me something about boundaries. A poem gives me a better model of choice. A conversation changes what I thought the problem was.

02 / The questions underneath

Stay with the question.

The first explanation is a place to begin. These are a few of the questions I tend to ask next.

I.What is the system actually rewarding?+

I want the architecture underneath the thing: incentives, assumptions, dependencies, and constraints. An outcome can look strange until you understand the conditions producing it.

II.Whose choices become possible?+

Human agency matters to me. Good help gives someone more information and more room to act for themselves, including the freedom to choose differently than I would.

I want convictions that can survive contact with questions. The odd detail, the exception, and the sentence that does not fit deserve attention.
